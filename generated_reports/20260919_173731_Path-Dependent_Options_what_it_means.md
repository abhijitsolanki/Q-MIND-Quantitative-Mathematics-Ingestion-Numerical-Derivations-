# 1. Executive Summary & Theoretical Intuition

Path-dependent options are derivative securities whose terminal payoffs, intermediate cash flows, or survival conditions depend fundamentally upon the entire trajectory of the underlying asset price path $\{S_u\}_{0 \le u \le T}$, rather than strictly on the terminal spot price $S_T$. 

In classical quantitative finance, standard European vanilla options are priced using the terminal filtration $\mathcal{F}_T^S$, meaning that their valuation depends exclusively on the state of the system at maturity, $V(S_T, T)$. Under the risk-neutral pricing measure $\mathbb{Q}$, the value is simply the discounted expectation of the terminal payoff:

$$V(S_t, t) = e^{-r(T-t)} \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \left[ \Phi(S_T) \mid \mathcal{F}_t \right]$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& V(S_t, t) \text{: Option value at time } t \text{, in currency units (\$)} \\
& e^{-r(T-t)} \text{: Stochastic discount factor under a deterministic constant risk-free rate, dimensionless} \\
& \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \text{: Conditional expectation operator under the risk-neutral probability measure } \mathbb{Q} \text{ given filtration } \mathcal{F}_t \\
& \Phi(S_T) \text{: Terminal payoff function of the option, in currency units (\$)} \\
& S_T \text{: Spot price of the underlying asset at maturity } T \text{, in currency units per share (\$/share)} \\
& r \text{: Risk-free interest rate, per annum (dimensionless fraction)} \\
& t \text{: Current time, in years} \\
& T \text{: Option maturity or expiration time, in years}
\end{aligned}
$$

Conversely, path-dependent options rely on the natural filtration generated by the entire price trajectory up to time $t$, denoted by $\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(\{S_u\}_{0 \le u \le t})$. Consequently, their payoff functions incorporate historical extrema, time-weighted averages, or survival criteria over sub-intervals of $[0, T]$. This structural difference requires expanding the mathematical modeling apparatus from standard one-dimensional Black-Scholes partial differential equations (PDEs) to multidimensional PDEs or specialized Monte Carlo path simulations.

# 2. Mathematical Framework & Underlying Assumptions

We model the underlying asset price $S_t$ under the risk-neutral pricing measure $\mathbb{Q}$ as a geometric Brownian motion (GBM):

$$dS_t = r S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& dS_t \text{: Infinitesimal increment of the spot asset price, in currency units per share (\$/share)} \\
& r \text{: Instantaneous risk-free interest rate, per annum (dimensionless fraction)} \\
& S_t \text{: Spot price of the underlying asset at time } t \text{, in currency units per share (\$/share)} \\
& dt \text{: Infinitesimal time increment, in years} \\
& \sigma \text{: Volatility of the underlying asset returns, per annum (dimensionless fraction)} \\
& dW_t \text{: Increment of a standard Brownian motion under measure } \mathbb{Q} \text{, in units of } \sqrt{\text{years}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The stochastic differential equation (SDE) governing $S_t$ assumes that the drift equals the risk-free rate $r$ (consistent with no-arbitrage pricing theory) and that the volatility parameter $\sigma$ remains strictly constant over the life of the contract.

Path-dependent options are partitioned into two primary classes based on how their trajectory dependence impacts the dimensionality of the governing valuation equation:

1. **Weak Path Dependency:** The trajectory matters (e.g., whether a barrier $H$ was ever breached), but the pricing problem can still be solved using the standard two-variable state space $(S_t, t)$. This is handled by imposing specialized internal boundary conditions, domain restrictions, or jump conditions rather than introducing an independent dynamic state variable.
2. **Strong Path Dependency:** The payoff depends on an integrated or extremal characteristic of the path (e.g., the running average $A_t$ or running maximum $M_t$) that evolves dynamically over time. This requires augmenting the state space with an additional auxiliary variable, transforming the pricing problem into a multi-dimensional system (e.g., $V(S_t, A_t, t)$).

# 3. Step-by-Step Derivation & Formula Mechanics

To illustrate strong path dependency, we examine the continuous-time **Asian Option** (fixed strike call). The contract payoff is determined by the arithmetic average of the asset price over the life of the option:

$$\text{Payoff} = \max \left( A_T - K, 0 \right)$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& A_T \text{: Terminal arithmetic average of the asset price over interval } [0, T] \text{, in currency units (\$/share)} \\
& K \text{: Strike price of the option, in currency units (\$/share)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The continuous running average $A_t$ at any time $t \in [0, T]$ is defined as:

$$A_t = \frac{1}{t} \int_0^t S_u du$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& A_t \text{: Running arithmetic average of the asset price up to time } t \text{, in currency units (\$/share)} \\
& u \text{: Integration dummy variable representing past time, in years} \\
& S_u \text{: Spot price of the underlying asset at past time } u \text{, in currency units per share (\$/share)}
\end{aligned}
$$

To formulate the governing PDE, we apply standard Itô calculus. First, differentiate the integral definition of $A_t$ with respect to time $t$. Note that $t A_t = \int_0^t S_u du$. Differentiating both sides with respect to $t$ using the Leibniz integral rule yields:

$$\frac{d}{dt} (t A_t) = S_t \implies t \frac{dA_t}{dt} + A_t \frac{dt}{dt} = S_t \implies t dAt + A_t dt = S_t dt$$

Rearranging for the differential $dA_t$:

$$dA_t = \frac{S_t - A_t}{t} dt$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& dA_t \text{: Infinitesimal increment of the running average, in currency units per share (\$/share)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Observe that $dA_t$ is locally deterministic given $S_t$ and $A_t$; it contains no stochastic $dW_t$ term. 

Next, let $V(S_t, A_t, t)$ represent the value of the Asian option. We expand $V$ using multidimensional Itô's Lemma for the two state variables $(S_t, A_t)$ governed by their respective SDEs:

$$dS_t = r S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t$$
$$dA_t = \left( \frac{S_t - A_t}{t} \right) dt$$

Applying Itô's Lemma for a function $V(S_t, A_t, t)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
dV &= \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} dt + \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} dS_t + \frac{\partial V}{\partial A} dA_t + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} (dS_t)^2 + \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S \partial A} dS_t dA_t + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial A^2} (dA_t)^2
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the differential rules ($(dS_t)^2 = \sigma^2 S_t^2 dt$, $dS_t dA_t = 0$ due to $dt \cdot dt = 0$ and $dt \cdot dW_t = 0$, and $(dA_t)^2 = 0$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
dV &= \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} dt + \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} \left( r S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t \right) + \frac{\partial V}{\partial A} \left( \frac{S_t - A_t}{t} \right) dt \\
&\quad + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} \sigma^2 S_t^2 dt
\end{aligned}
$$

Grouping the $dt$ and $dW_t$ terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
dV &= \left[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + r S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} + \frac{S_t - A_t}{t} \frac{\partial V}{\partial A} + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} \right] dt + \sigma S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} dW_t
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& dV \text{: Infinitesimal change in the option value, in currency units (\$)} \\
& \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} \text{: Partial derivative of option value with respect to time, in currency units per year (\$/year)} \\
& \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} \text{: Delta of the option with respect to spot price, dimensionless} \\
& \frac{\partial V}{\partial A} \text{: Sensitivity of the option value to the running average, dimensionless} \\
& \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} \text{: Gamma of the option with respect to spot price, in shares per currency unit (\$/share)}^{-1} \\
& \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial A^2} \text{: Second partial derivative with respect to the running average, (\$/share)}^{-1} \\
& \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S \partial A} \text{: Cross-derivative with respect to spot price and running average, (\$/share)}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

To price this derivative via risk-neutral valuation, we construct a locally risk-free portfolio $\Pi$ consisting of the option $V$, delta shares of the underlying asset $\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}$, and delta-average units of an auxiliary hedging instrument. By standard arbitrage arguments under the risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$, the return on this hedged portfolio must equal the risk-free rate $r \Pi dt$. This eliminates the Brownian increment $dW_t$, yielding the governing two-dimensional linear partial differential equation for the Asian option:

$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + r S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} + \left( \frac{S_t - A_t}{t} \right) \frac{\partial V}{\partial A} + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} = r V$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{Variable Dictionary:} \\
& \Pi \text{: Value of the self-financing hedging portfolio, in currency units (\$)}
\end{aligned}
$$

This PDE must be solved subject to the terminal condition:

$$V(S_T, A_T, T) = \max(A_T - K, 0)$$

# 5. Worked Numerical Financial Example (Step-by-Step)

Consider a discretely monitored **Asian Call Option** with fixed strike to illustrate path-dependent simulation pricing. 

### Given Parameters:
* Current Spot Price: $S_0 = \$100.00$
* Strike Price: $K = \$100.00$
* Time to Maturity: $T = 1.0 \text{ year}$
* Risk-Free Rate: $r = 0.05 \text{ (5\% per annum)}$
* Volatility: $\sigma = 0.20 \text{ (20\% per annum)}$
* Monitoring Frequency: $m = 4 \text{ quarterly steps}$ ($t_1 = 0.25, t_2 = 0.50, t_3 = 0.75, t_4 = 1.00$)

### Step 1: Simulate a Single Asset Path Under Measure $\mathbb{Q}$
The discrete GBM simulation formula over time step $\Delta t = 0.25$ is:
$$S_{t_{i}} = S_{t_{i-1}} \exp\left( \left( r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \right)\Delta t + \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} Z_i \right)$$
where $Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ are independent standard normal random variables.

Assume a generated sequence of standard normal draws:
* $Z_1 = +0.50$
* $Z_2 = -0.20$
* $Z_3 = +1.10$
* $Z_4 = -0.80$

Calculate the drift-volatility exponent component:
$$\left( r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 \right)\Delta t = \left( 0.05 - \frac{1}{2}(0.2)^2 \right) (0.25) = (0.05 - 0.02)(0.25) = (0.03)(0.25) = 0.0075$$
$$\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} = (0.20)\sqrt{0.25} = (0.20)(0.5) = 0.10$$
